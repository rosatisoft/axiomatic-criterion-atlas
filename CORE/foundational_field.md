# ACA — Axiomatic Criterion Atlas

## Foundational Field

### Operational Semantic Foundation for Criterion Preservation

---

## 1. Purpose

The Foundational Field defines the primary semantic reference layer used by ACA to preserve criterion during semantic trajectory evolution.

ACA does not begin from isolated prompts, keywords, or post-generation validation. It begins by observing the field under analysis, identifying the invariants required for stable evaluation, and evaluating whether a trajectory preserves or destabilizes those invariants.

The Foundational Field functions as the base orientation layer from which higher fields may derive contextual stability.

It does not claim metaphysical infallibility, universal truth detection, or moral omniscience. It defines an operational reference structure for criterion-preserving semantic evaluation.

---

## 2. Operational Definition

A foundational field is a semantic field composed of invariants whose preservation is necessary for coherent criterion continuity inside a declared origin and context.

In ACA:

```text
Foundational Field =
semantic subspace generated by foundational invariants
```

The field defines the minimum geometric conditions required for a semantic system to remain oriented while evolving through contextual transformations.

## 6. Criterion Preservation

Criterion preservation means that a reasoning trajectory remains directionally aligned with the invariant structures that define the active field.

ACA separates:

```text
Contextual Compatibility
from
Epistemic Orientation
```

Contextual compatibility evaluates whether a trajectory belongs to a field.

Epistemic orientation evaluates whether the trajectory preserves or inverts the criterion of that field.

A system may remain locally coherent while progressively weakening or inverting the invariant structures that originally stabilized interpretation.

This failure is called criterion drift.

## 12. Foundational Formula

Let:

```text
C = [v1, v2, ..., vk]
```

be a context matrix generated from foundational invariant anchors.

Let:

```text
S = span(C)
```

be the semantic field generated by those anchors.

For an input embedding z, contextual compatibility is measured by origin cost:

```text
O(z) = || z - ΠS(z) ||²
```

However, contextual compatibility alone is insufficient.

ACA also evaluates directional orientation relative to invariant criterion vectors.

Thus, a trajectory is stable only when it preserves both:

```text
low origin cost
+
positive epistemic orientation
```

---

## 13. Central Instability Condition

Criterion drift occurs when:

```text
O(z) is low
```

but:

```text
epistemic orientation weakens or becomes negative
```

Meaning:

```text
The statement still belongs to the field,
but it is beginning to weaken or invert the criterion of the field.
```

This is the central problem ACA is designed to detect.

At this stage, ACA treats drift as the observable signal of orientation loss. Broader measures such as orientational entropy may be explored later, after origin, context, and invariant relevance are operationally defined.

---

## 14. Foundational Principle

The Foundational Field is the first semantic structure of ACA because higher reasoning depends on stable orientation.

Without foundational invariants, semantic systems may remain fluent while becoming directionally unstable.

ACA therefore begins with the following operational principle:

```text
A generative system cannot preserve reliable reasoning
unless it preserves the invariant structures
that give reasoning its direction.
```
