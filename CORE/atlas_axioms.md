\# ACA — Atlas Axioms



\## Origin of Criterion



ACA defines criterion as the capacity to preserve orientation during semantic trajectory evolution.



ACA does not impose a metaphysical doctrine or claim universal truth detection. It begins from an operational requirement: before a system can evaluate, infer, or act reliably, it must preserve stable reference structures within a declared origin and context.



The scientific starting point of ACA is therefore:



1\. observe the field under analysis;

2\. identify the invariants required for stable evaluation;

3\. define the active context and origin of criterion;

4\. evaluate whether semantic trajectories preserve or destabilize those invariants.



\## Foundational Field



The Foundational Field is divided into two operational cores.



\### Logical-Epistemic Core



This core establishes the minimum conditions for reality-based evaluation:



\- identity

\- non\_contradiction

\- correspondence

\- evidence\_constraint

\- causal\_continuity

\- uncertainty\_preservation



These invariants preserve reference, consistency, evidence, correspondence, causal continuity, and the limits of justified certainty.



\### Operational-Orientational Core



This core preserves stable semantic operation within a declared origin and context:



\- persistence

\- relation

\- semantic\_stability

\- interpretive\_constraint

\- field\_boundary

\- orientation\_continuity



These invariants preserve meaning, relation, context, field boundaries, interpretive discipline, and continuity of orientation.



\## Principle vs Rule



ACA distinguishes principles from rules.



A rule controls a specific action.



A principle establishes the point of reference from which actions can be evaluated.



ACA therefore does not define criterion as a list of fixed rules. It defines criterion as an invariant reference structure that allows trajectories to be evaluated across changing contexts.



\## Orientation



Orientation cannot be measured independently of origin and context.



A trajectory is evaluated only after the active field, relevant invariants, and contextual conditions have been identified.



Criterion preservation requires both contextual compatibility and directional invariant preservation.



\## Drift



Criterion drift occurs when a trajectory remains semantically compatible with a field while progressively weakening or inverting the invariants that preserve orientation.



At this stage, ACA treats drift as the observable signal of orientation loss. Broader measures such as orientational entropy may be explored later, after origin, context, and invariant relevance are operationally defined.
