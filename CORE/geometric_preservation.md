# ACA — Axiomatic Criterion Atlas

## Geometric Preservation

### Preservation of Criterion Through Semantic Trajectory Evolution

---

## 1. Purpose

Geometric Preservation defines the stability condition of ACA.

A semantic trajectory is not reliable only because it remains close to a contextual field. It is reliable when it preserves both semantic location and semantic direction across trajectory evolution.

In ACA:

```text
geometric preservation =
contextual compatibility
+
positive epistemic orientation
+
trajectory preservation
```

This means that a trajectory must remain compatible with the active field while preserving the invariant structures that give that field its criterion.

---

## 2. Origin and Context Requirement

Geometric preservation cannot be interpreted without origin and context.

Before preservation can be evaluated, ACA must identify:

* declared origin of criterion;
* active semantic field;
* relevant context;
* applicable invariants;
* trajectory state or sequence under evaluation.

Without origin and context, origin cost and orientation scores may be mathematically computable but operationally underdetermined.

Therefore:

```text
geometric preservation requires declared origin and context
```

ACA does not treat preservation as an abstract score. It treats preservation as a field-relative condition evaluated within a declared origin, context, and invariant structure.

---

## 3. Foundational Principle

ACA proposes:

```text
Reliable reasoning requires preservation
of semantic location
and
semantic direction.
```

Semantic location determines whether a trajectory belongs to a field.

Semantic direction determines whether the trajectory preserves the criterion of that field.

A trajectory may remain close to a field while progressively weakening or inverting the invariant structures that preserve criterion.

---

## 4. Relationship to Foundational Cores

Geometric preservation is evaluated relative to the foundational invariant structure of ACA.

The foundational layer is divided into two operational cores:

```text
Foundational Invariants =
Logical-Epistemic Core
+
Operational-Orientational Core
```

The Logical-Epistemic Core contains invariants required for reality-based evaluation:

* identity
* non_contradiction
* correspondence
* evidence_constraint
* causal_continuity
* uncertainty_preservation

The Operational-Orientational Core contains invariants required for stable semantic operation within a declared origin and context:

* persistence
* relation
* semantic_stability
* interpretive_constraint
* field_boundary
* orientation_continuity

Geometric preservation requires that the active trajectory preserve the relevant invariants for its declared field and context.

---

## 5. Semantic Field Compatibility

Let:

```text
S ⊆ R^d
```

be a semantic field generated by invariant anchors.

Let:

```text
z_t ∈ R^d
```

be the semantic state of a trajectory at time `t`.

Contextual compatibility is measured by origin cost:

```text
O(z_t) = || z_t - ΠS(z_t) ||²
```

where:

```text
ΠS(z_t)
```

is the projection of `z_t` onto semantic field `S`.

Low origin cost means the trajectory remains geometrically compatible with the field.

However, contextual compatibility alone is insufficient for criterion preservation.

---

## 6. Directional Preservation

Let:

```text
D = {d_1, d_2, ..., d_n}
```

be the system of directional invariants.

Aggregate orientation is defined as:

```text
Phi(z_t) = sum_i w_i * phi_i(z_t)
```

where:

```text
phi_i(z_t) = <normalize(z_t), d_i>
```

and:

* `w_i` are invariant relevance weights;
* `d_i` are directional invariant vectors;
* `phi_i(z_t)` is the directional projection of `z_t` onto invariant `d_i`.

A trajectory preserves criterion when:

```text
Phi(z_t) >= theta_Phi
```

for the relevant invariant structure.

Positive orientation indicates criterion preservation.

Near-zero orientation indicates ambiguity or weak orientation.

Negative orientation indicates directional inversion.

---

## 7. Preservation Condition

ACA defines geometric preservation as:

```text
for all t:
O(z_t) <= theta_O
and
Phi(z_t) >= theta_Phi
```

This means that every trajectory state must remain both:

* contextually compatible;
* directionally oriented.

Thus:

```text
preserved trajectory =
low origin cost
+
positive epistemic orientation
+
stable trajectory evolution
```

---

## 8. Preservation Failure

Geometric preservation fails when either condition breaks.

### Contextual Failure

```text
O(z_t) > theta_O
```

The trajectory leaves the active semantic field or becomes insufficiently compatible with it.

### Directional Failure

```text
Phi(z_t) < theta_Phi
```

The trajectory may remain inside the field but loses criterion orientation.

The second case is more subtle and defines the core problem of ACA.

A system may remain fluent, plausible, and semantically close while progressively weakening or inverting the invariant structures that preserve criterion.

---

## 9. Criterion Drift Condition

Criterion drift occurs when:

```text
O(z_t) <= theta_O
```

while:

```text
Phi(z_t) < 0
```

Meaning:

```text
the trajectory remains semantically compatible
but directionally inverted
```

A weaker early drift condition may occur when:

```text
Delta Phi_t < 0
```

persists across trajectory evolution.

This allows ACA to detect loss of preservation before full inversion occurs.

---

## 10. Preservation Over Time

Let:

```text
T = {z_1, z_2, ..., z_T}
```

be a semantic trajectory.

ACA evaluates preservation across the full trajectory, not only at isolated points.

Orientation decay is defined as:

```text
Delta Phi_t = Phi(z_t) - Phi(z_{t-1})
```

Persistent negative decay indicates loss of criterion stability.

Trajectory preservation therefore requires:

* contextual compatibility over time;
* positive or sufficient orientation over time;
* absence of persistent directional decay;
* explicit recognition of field boundary changes.

---

## 11. Stability Classes

ACA distinguishes the following states:

| State              | Condition                                                        |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Stable             | low origin cost and positive orientation                         |
| Weak               | low origin cost and weak orientation                             |
| Ambiguous          | unclear field or near-zero orientation                           |
| Drifting           | orientation decay across time                                    |
| Inverted           | low origin cost and negative orientation                         |
| Dispersed          | high origin cost                                                 |
| Underdefined       | origin, context, or active field is insufficiently declared      |
| Foundational Drift | instability originates from the goal, origin, or criterion frame |

These classes allow ACA to separate ordinary contextual uncertainty from directional loss of criterion.

---

## 12. Geometric Preservation and Runtime Systems

Runtime systems may use preservation metrics to determine:

* whether to allow reasoning;
* whether to constrain reasoning;
* whether to request clarification;
* whether to monitor trajectory decay;
* whether to flag criterion drift;
* whether to request origin or context;
* whether to escalate or reframe the task.

Possible runtime policies:

| Condition                      | Action                 |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| preserved                      | ALLOW                  |
| weak preservation              | ALLOW_LIGHT            |
| ambiguity                      | CLARIFY                |
| decay                          | MONITOR                |
| inversion                      | FLAG_DRIFT             |
| dispersion                     | REJECT_OR_CLARIFY      |
| underdefined origin or context | REQUEST_ORIGIN_CONTEXT |
| foundational drift             | ESCALATE_OR_REFRAME    |

These actions preserve the distinction between semantic compatibility and criterion preservation.

---

## 13. Drift as Observable Signal

At this stage, ACA treats observed drift as the primary signal of orientation loss.

ACA does not yet require a formal metric of orientational entropy.

Instead, it evaluates observable destabilization through:

* contradiction;
* rhetorical substitution;
* evidential weakening;
* loss of correspondence;
* uncertainty converted into certainty;
* field boundary crossing;
* orientation decay;
* invariant inversion.

Future work may formalize orientational entropy as a broader measure of instability after origin, context, field selection, and invariant relevance are operationally defined.

Together, these layers form the criterion geometry of ACA.

---

## 15. Foundational Principle

ACA proposes:

```text
A semantic system is stable only when it preserves
both the field it belongs to
and the criterion that gives that field direction.
```

Therefore:

```text
reliable reasoning =
semantic compatibility
+
epistemic orientation
+
trajectory preservation
```

Without geometric preservation:

* semantic proximity becomes insufficient;
* contextual coherence may conceal destabilization;
* fluent reasoning may lose criterion direction;
* trajectories may remain plausible while weakening the invariants that preserve evaluation.

Geometric preservation is therefore the operational stability condition of ACA.
