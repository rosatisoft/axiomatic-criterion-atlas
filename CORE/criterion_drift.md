# ACA — Axiomatic Criterion Atlas

## Criterion Drift

### Observable Loss of Direction Inside Coherent Semantic Space

---

## 1. Purpose

Criterion Drift defines the central instability phenomenon modeled by ACA.

ACA proposes that semantic systems may preserve contextual compatibility while progressively losing epistemic orientation.

This produces a critical distinction:

```text
semantic compatibility
does not necessarily imply
criterion preservation
```

Criterion drift occurs when a trajectory remains geometrically compatible with a semantic field while weakening, displacing, or inverting the invariant structures that define the criterion of that field.

At this stage, ACA treats drift as the primary observable signal of orientation loss. Broader concepts such as orientational entropy may be explored later, after origin, context, field relevance, and invariant weighting are operationally defined.

---

## 2. Origin and Context Requirement

Criterion drift cannot be interpreted in isolation.

Before drift can be evaluated, ACA must identify:

* declared origin of criterion;
* active semantic field;
* relevant context;
* applicable invariants;
* trajectory state or sequence under evaluation.

Without origin and context, a trajectory may be mathematically projected into a field, but its orientation remains operationally underdetermined.

Therefore:

```text
drift evaluation requires declared origin and context
```

ACA does not treat drift as an abstract moral score. It treats drift as a field-relative signal that a trajectory may be weakening or inverting the invariants required for criterion preservation.

---

## 3. Foundational Hypothesis

Traditional semantic systems primarily evaluate:

* similarity;
* contextual overlap;
* semantic clustering;
* probabilistic continuation;
* local coherence.

ACA proposes that these measurements are insufficient.

A trajectory may remain:

* coherent;
* persuasive;
* grammatically stable;
* semantically compatible;
* close to a contextual field;

while progressively destabilizing the orientation that originally constrained interpretation.

Thus:

```text
criterion drift =
directional semantic destabilization
inside coherent contextual space
```

---

## 4. Relationship to Foundational Cores

Criterion drift is evaluated relative to the foundational invariant structure of ACA.

The foundational layer is divided into two operational cores:

```text
Foundational Invariants =
Logical-Epistemic Core
+
Operational-Orientational Core
```

The Logical-Epistemic Core contains invariants required for reality-based evaluation:

* identity
* non_contradiction
* correspondence
* evidence_constraint
* causal_continuity
* uncertainty_preservation

The Operational-Orientational Core contains invariants required for stable semantic operation within a declared origin and context:

* persistence
* relation
* semantic_stability
* interpretive_constraint
* field_boundary
* orientation_continuity

Criterion drift may emerge through destabilization of either core.

Logical-epistemic drift weakens evaluation of reality, evidence, consistency, correspondence, causal continuity, or justified uncertainty.

Operational-orientational drift weakens meaning, relation, field awareness, interpretive discipline, or continuity of orientation.

---

## 5. Contextual Compatibility vs Criterion Preservation

ACA formally separates two dimensions.

| Dimension                | Meaning                                                      |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Contextual Compatibility | whether a trajectory belongs to a field                      |
| Epistemic Orientation    | whether the trajectory preserves criterion inside that field |

A system may preserve one while losing the other.

This distinction is essential because a trajectory may remain contextually coherent while progressively destabilizing the criterion that gives the field its direction.

---

## 6. Semantic Field Compatibility

Let:

```text
S ⊆ R^d
```

represent the active semantic field.

Let:

```text
z_t ∈ R^d
```

represent the trajectory embedding at state `t`.

ACA defines contextual compatibility using origin cost:

```text
O(z_t) = || z_t - ΠS(z_t) ||²
```

where:

```text
ΠS(z_t)
```

is the projection onto semantic field `S`.

Low origin cost indicates semantic compatibility with the field.

However, contextual compatibility alone is insufficient.

---

## 7. Directional Orientation

Let:

```text
D = {d_1, d_2, ..., d_n}
```

represent the directional invariant system.

ACA defines aggregate epistemic orientation as:

```text
Phi(z_t) = sum_i w_i * phi_i(z_t)
```

where:

```text
phi_i(z_t) = <normalize(z_t), d_i>
```

and:

* `w_i` are invariant relevance weights;
* `d_i` are directional invariant vectors;
* positive orientation preserves criterion;
* near-zero orientation indicates ambiguity or weak orientation;
* negative orientation indicates directional inversion.

The relevance of each invariant depends on declared origin, active field, context, and trajectory type.

---

## 8. Formal Definition of Criterion Drift

ACA defines criterion drift as the condition where contextual compatibility is preserved while orientation weakens, decays, or becomes negative.

A strong drift condition occurs when:

```text
O(z_t) <= theta_O
```

while simultaneously:

```text
Phi(z_t) < 0
```

Meaning:

```text
the trajectory still belongs to the field,
but no longer preserves the field's criterion
```

A weaker early drift condition may occur when:

```text
O(z_t) <= theta_O
```

while:

```text
Delta Phi_t < 0
```

persistently across trajectory states.

This allows ACA to detect drift before full directional inversion occurs.

---

## 9. Progressive Drift

Criterion drift frequently emerges gradually rather than catastrophically.

Drift may appear through:

* rhetorical reinterpretation;
* emotional substitution;
* semantic reframing;
* contradiction normalization;
* evidential weakening;
* contextual displacement;
* uncertainty converted into certainty;
* correspondence loss;
* unnoticed field crossing;
* interpretive expansion beyond context;
* progressive reinterpretive pressure.

A trajectory may appear semantically coherent while progressively losing orientation.

---

## 10. Drift Dynamics

ACA models semantic evolution as a trajectory:

```text
T = {z_1, z_2, ..., z_t}
```

where each state evolves relative to:

* contextual geometry;
* semantic attractors;
* invariant structures;
* directional orientation;
* declared origin and context.

Criterion drift emerges when orientation progressively decays across trajectory evolution.

---

## 11. Orientation Decay

Let:

```text
Phi_t = Phi(z_t)
```

represent orientation at trajectory state `t`.

ACA defines orientation decay as:

```text
Delta Phi_t = Phi_t - Phi_{t-1}
```

Persistent negative decay indicates:

```text
directional destabilization
```

even before explicit inversion occurs.

Orientation decay is therefore one of the earliest observable signs of possible criterion drift.

| Region           | Description                       |

These regions may emerge even when contextual coherence remains partially preserved.

## 13. Hidden Drift

One of the most important observations of ACA is that criterion drift may remain hidden under semantic coherence.

Examples include:

* persuasive factual reinterpretation;
* emotionally coherent contradiction;
* rhetorical inversion;
* narrative substitution;
* semantically plausible manipulation;
* uncertainty presented as certainty;
* coherent claims detached from evidence;
* correspondence weakened under fluent language.

Thus:

```text
semantic fluency
does not guarantee
epistemic stability
```

```text
directional semantic geometry
```

## 16. Foundational Drift

Criterion drift usually describes loss of orientation inside an active field.

A stronger condition may occur when instability appears to originate from the declared goal, source of orientation, or criterion frame itself.

ACA identifies this as foundational drift.

```text
foundational drift =
instability in the origin, goal, or criterion frame
that guides the trajectory
```

In this case, ACA should not only evaluate the generated trajectory. It should question whether the origin, goal, or active frame is sufficiently defined and compatible with the invariant structure required for stable evaluation.

Foundational drift does not require a metaphysical judgment. It indicates that the system cannot reliably evaluate orientation because the origin or context is unstable, contradictory, underdefined, or incompatible with the active invariants.

```text
criterion-aware runtime control
```

inside generative systems.

## 18. Drift vs Hallucination

ACA does not reduce criterion drift to hallucination.

Hallucination typically refers to:

* unsupported claims;
* fabricated information;
* factual inaccuracy.

Criterion drift is broader.

A trajectory may remain factually plausible while directionally destabilizing the invariant structures that preserve criterion.

Thus:

```text
criterion drift
is a geometric epistemic phenomenon
not merely factual error
```

## 19. Drift as Observable Signal

At this stage, ACA treats observed drift as the primary signal of orientation loss.

ACA does not yet require a formal metric of orientational entropy.

Instead, it evaluates observable drift through:

* contradiction;
* rhetorical substitution;
* evidential weakening;
* loss of correspondence;
* field boundary crossing;
* uncertainty inflation;
* orientation decay;
* invariant inversion.

Future work may formalize orientational entropy as a broader measure of instability once origin, context, field selection, and invariant relevance are operationally defined.

## 21. Foundational Principle

ACA proposes the following principle:

```text
Reliable semantic systems require persistent preservation
of invariant orientation during trajectory evolution.
```

Without criterion preservation:

* semantic proximity becomes insufficient;
* contextual coherence may conceal destabilization;
* generative systems may progressively invert their own foundational structures while remaining locally coherent;
* the system may appear fluent while losing the criterion that gives reasoning its direction.

Therefore:

```text
reliable reasoning requires
both semantic compatibility
and directional criterion preservation
```
